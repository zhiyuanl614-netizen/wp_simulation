# Early-Warning-Driven Proactive Resilience Optimization of Urban Water–Power Cyber-Physical Systems

---

# Abstract

**Abstract:** A coal-fired unit draws its circulating cooling make-up water from the municipal distribution network, so the two infrastructures are coupled physically and through their control systems. Interdependence studies replace this plant-side process with a direct coupling edge, and climate-impact studies address only slow, weather-driven derating; the fast, fault-driven chain remains unmodeled. This paper models the complete fault-driven chain: municipal head decay, make-up interruption, in-plant storage depletion, circulating-pump derating, and back-pressure rise to the protection trip. A cross-domain early warning then drives LP-based proactive optimization on a real 399-node urban network coupled to IEEE-118 through six designated plant-intake pairs (2,631 MW). The warning removes the single-unit deficit entirely, from 485.6 MW and 49.1 MWh to zero. It keeps all 24 multi-unit failure subsets at zero deficit, the buffer windows of 92.4–192.9 min far exceeding the ~15 min full reserve deployment. A critical ramp-down rate of 0.545\% $P_g$/min exploits a derating self-protection feedback and extends unit running time to 1.98 times the window. The passive deficit saturates at 12–13 MWh independent of failure size, placing the applicability boundary on reserve adequacy and ramping capability rather than failure scale. The resilience bottleneck of water–power systems thus shifts from information speed to reserve sufficiency under multi-unit common-origin losses.

**Keywords:** interdependent infrastructure; urban water networks; cooling-water failure cascade; cross-domain early warning; proactive control; reserve adequacy

**Highlights:**

- Fault-driven cooling-water chain modeled for urban water-power early warning
- Early-warning-driven proactive optimization: 485.6 MW deficit; 24/24 cases zero
- Plant cooling buffers span 92-193 min across six coupled units
- Critical ramp-down rate extends running time 1.98x via derating feedback

# 1 Introduction

Between the municipal water network and the power system sits an intermediate system that is usually simplified away: the circulating cooling water of a power plant. A conventional coal-fired unit loses circulating water continuously to evaporation, blowdown, and wind drift. The make-up flow that compensates for these losses is drawn from the municipal distribution network. When the city side fails, the plant does not lose cooling at once. It drains its tanks and sump first, derates, and only then trips. The two infrastructures are therefore **coupled twice, physically through the cooling water and informationally through the industrial control systems (ICS/SCADA)** [1–3]. This double coupling forms an urban water–power cyber-physical system (CPS) whose failure physics has rarely been modeled at the process level.

Under heatwaves and droughts, thermal and nuclear units in Europe and the United States have been derated or shut down as cooling-water temperatures climbed and river flows dropped [4–9]. Conversely, pipe bursts, treatment-plant failures, and blackouts [10] can sever the municipal make-up of several units at once — a **common-cause failure**. Interdependence studies mostly replace the intermediate cooling-water process with a direct coupling edge [11–14]. Ouyang [14] systematizes the methodology space; Li and Zhang [12] couple a real water network with a standard power system; Wang et al. [13] federate multiple infrastructures. The cooling-water dependence line [4–6, 15–17] quantifies slow, weather-driven derating from a siting-and-planning perspective. In condenser engineering, ε-NTU exchange and back-pressure characteristics rest on mature standards [18–20], yet they have not entered a system-level failure chain in grid resilience analysis [21–24]. Closest to this paper, Yu et al. [25] propose early-warning indicators and proactive LP control for the gas–power system.

Across these strands, three limitations overlap. First, existing work models the **outcome** of cooling-water loss and skips the **process** — the in-plant chain from depressurization to trip. Second, it addresses **slow**, weather-driven derating and misses **fast** fault cascades [26–28]. Third, the early-warning paradigm stops at **gas–power**; water sits in lumped plant-side vessels, whereas gas linepack is distributed across the network. The exploitable buffer time between depressurization and the trip is ignored in all three lines. Three questions follow. How slowly does a municipal supply failure propagate through the plant cooling chain into a power deficit? Can the time margin be exploited, before the unit trips, by cross-domain information and proactive control? And where is the physical boundary of that exploitation?

The core idea is a timescale conversion. The cooling-water buffer separates municipal depressurization from the unit trip by minutes to hours, while information systems and grid controls respond in seconds. If this slow–fast margin is converted, through cross-domain early warning, into a proactive control window, the water-side fault can be hedged before it matures into a power deficit. Everything that follows serves this single conversion: quantifying the margin, exploiting it, and bounding the exploitation. To this end, the study builds a three-layer model — municipal network, plant cooling chain, and power system with proactive control — and stresses it on a real coupled testbed.

The contributions are fourfold. First, this is the first early-warning resilience study of interdependent water–power systems that **models the complete cooling-water process explicitly**, from municipal head decay to unit trip (Chapter 2). Second, it **quantifies and exploits the slow–fast margin**: CWR/UET/SUET indicators, a three-domain ICS warning chain, and early-warning-driven LP optimization (PA/SP/DP). The analysis reveals a critical ramp-down rate, $r^{*} = 0.545\%\,P_g/\mathrm{min}$, whose self-protection feedback extends the buffer window to 1.98× the SUET (Chapters 2 and 4). Third, it delivers **a real-network boundary with auditability**: the D-town network, deterministic pairing into six designated pairs (re-verified, 6/6), and position and water-balance checks (Chapters 3 and 5). Fourth, it draws **conclusions with an explicit boundary**: across all 24 k = 1–6 subsets, warning-driven control keeps the deficit at zero. The passive deficit saturates at 12–13 MWh from k = 2 onward; the boundary lies on the reserve-and-ramping axis, $R^{*}$ and $r_f^{*}$ (Chapters 4 and 5). The cross-domain finding is that the resilience bottleneck shifts from information speed to reserve adequacy and ramping capability.

# 2 Methodology

This chapter develops the mathematical model of the four-layer physical chain and its information layer under three principles: **mechanistic white-box modeling** (classical physical relations only — orifice hydraulics, ε-NTU heat transfer, the Antoine relation, the DC power flow), **one-to-one correspondence between equations and implementation** (every equation has exactly one code implementation), and **timescale separation** (seconds: electromechanics; minutes: hydraulics and thermal behavior; hours: municipal decay). The chain carries an inherent **time gap between the hours-scale cooling-water buffer and the seconds-scale information system and power controls**; §2.4 and §2.6 convert it into an actionable window.

## 2.1 Water–power cyber-physical coupling framework

The coupled system is decomposed along the fault-propagation direction into four physical layers and one information layer (Fig. 1; Supplementary Table S1): the municipal distribution layer, the plant cooling-water layer, the condenser–turbine layer, and the power system layer. The municipal layer — source, mains, district tanks, and plant intake nodes — is a boundary layer: it delivers the intake-head trajectory downstream and receives no feedback (verified in the case study). The district tanks are the primary buffer, hour-scale and position-dependent; the in-plant storage is the secondary buffer, minute-scale and unit-specific. Downstream, the attenuated circulating flow raises the back pressure through the correction and protection laws, the output changes propagate through the DC power flow and the reserve hierarchy with proactive control as an LP, and the dominant time scales shorten from hours to seconds along the chain.

The information layer is a three-domain (municipal, cooling, power), three-level (field control, data acquisition, dispatch) ICS following the federated co-simulation design of [13], modeled at the functional level (Chapter 6). Its only cross-domain message is the municipal head early warning, delivered with zero latency.

A unified event time axis is adopted: the origin $t=0$ is the municipal pressure failure at the intake nodes (Eq. (2.2)); tripping, reserve start-up, and the LP horizon are all measured relative to it.

| Information | Three-domain, three-level ICS | Threshold detection + cross-domain warning | Sub-second | Warning status; commands | Early warning signal → LP dispatch |

**Fig. 1 Overall framework of the water–power cyber-physical system**: ① the municipal water supply layer (single source – district storage-tank buffer – plant intake nodes; an intake head below 28 m constitutes the failure source) → the plant cooling-water layer (hydraulics, minutes) → the condenser/LP-turbine layer (thermo-mechanical, seconds–minutes) → the power system layer; the information layer on the right is a three-domain (municipal/cooling/power), three-level (PLC sensing – SCADA monitoring – dispatch decision) ICS, through which a municipal pressure violation reaches the power-system dispatcher as a zero-latency cross-domain early warning, forming the channel for exploiting the slow-dynamics/fast-response time margin.

## 2.2 Municipal water network model

The fault source lies in the municipal water network; this section derives the time at which each plant intake node loses its make-up supply after a sole-source outage.

The make-up water for the plant cooling system is driven by the pressure head at the intake node. This head is represented by the following parametric trajectory:

$$
H_{\mathrm{muni}}(t)=\begin{cases}
H_{\mathrm{muni},0}, & t<t_{\mathrm{fault}},\\[4pt]
H_{\mathrm{muni},0}\Big(1-\dfrac{t-t_{\mathrm{fault}}}{\tau_r}\Big)^{+}, & t\ge t_{\mathrm{fault}},\ \tau_r>0,\\[4pt]
0, & t\ge t_{\mathrm{fault}},\ \tau_r=0,
\end{cases}
\tag{2.1}
$$

where $H_{\mathrm{muni},0}$ is the nominal supply head, $(x)^{+}=\max(x,0)$, and $\tau_r$ the transition duration; $\tau_r=0$ defines the step caliber (B-ST), a positive value the gradual caliber (B-RT).

The failure time of the municipal supply is defined by the following criterion:

$$
t_{\mathrm{fail}}=\inf\bigl\{\,t\ge t_{\mathrm{fault}}:\ H_{\mathrm{muni}}(t)<H_{\min}\,\bigr\},
\qquad H_{\min}=28\ \mathrm{m},
\tag{2.2}
$$

Below the threshold the make-up channel fails, and $t_{\mathrm{fail}}$ is the origin of the event axis.

The parameters and the nodal failure times are generated by hydraulic simulation of the D-town network [29, 30] with EPANET 2.2, extended-period, pressure-driven demand [31–33] (15 min step, 72 h window).

The pressure-driven demand follows the Wagner-type square-root relation [34–36] ($p_{\min}=0$, $p_{\mathrm{req}}=20\ \mathrm{m}$; semantics in Supplementary S1.1):

$$
q_n = q_n^{\mathrm{req}}\cdot f(p_n),\qquad
f(p)=\min\Bigl(1,\ \sqrt{\bigl(\tfrac{p-p_{\min}}{p_{\mathrm{req}}-p_{\min}}\bigr)^{+}}\Bigr),
\tag{2.3}
$$

In the sole-source outage the reservoir head is zero and the city relies on the district tanks; each node's failure time is the first threshold crossing, latched, and the head trajectory is the complete plant-side input.

**Boundary usage map.** The synthetic trajectory bounds the three mechanism chains; the power-side LP reads only the real failure instants of the six intake nodes (Table 2), the real heads never driving the plant dynamics.

## 2.3 Cooling-water fault mechanism chain

Existing studies map a municipal supply failure directly to a unit trip [12], omitting the intermediate processes. This chain is complementary to the climate-derating line [4–6, 15–17] — that literature addresses slow weather-driven derating on planning timescales, this chain the fast fault-driven storage depletion — the two sharing only the condenser back-pressure physics of §2.3.2. The chain of Fig. 2 comprises a water-side hydraulic submodel (§2.3.1), a condenser and low-pressure turbine submodel (§2.3.2), and the early warning indicators (§2.3.3).

![Fig.2](figures/Fig2_cooling_water_chain.svg)

**Fig. 2 Physical process chain of the plant cooling-water system**: intake node (failure source, head below 28 m) → high-level make-up tank (mass balance) → collection sump (storage buffer) → circulating pump (NPSH and cavitation) → condenser (ε-NTU heat transfer) → low-pressure turbine (back pressure and derating $k_p$) → generator (output decline or trip), with cooling-tower heat rejection and three circulating losses; the dark-shaded links denote the intermediate processes omitted in existing studies.

### 2.3.1 Water-side hydraulic model

The water side consists of a high-level make-up tank, a collection sump, and the circulating pumps; all flows follow an orifice-type law — coefficient × opening × √driving head. Dimensions and values follow two Chinese design standards (Supplementary S1.2).

**Municipal make-up flow.** The make-up flow and its valve command are

$$
Q_{\mathrm{make}}(t)=C_v^{\mathrm{m}}\,\nu_{\mathrm{make}}(t)\sqrt{\bigl(H_{\mathrm{muni}}(t)-H_t(t)\bigr)^{+}}\cdot\mathbf{1}\bigl[t<t_{\mathrm{fail}}\bigr],
\tag{2.4}
$$

$$
\nu_{\mathrm{make}}(t)=\max\Bigl(\mathrm{clip}\bigl(K_T\bigl(H_T^{\mathrm{set}}-H_t\bigr),\,0,\,1\bigr),\ \ 0.02\cdot\mathbf{1}\bigl[H_t<H_T^{\mathrm{set}}\bigr]\Bigr),
\tag{2.5}
$$

where $C_v^{\mathrm{m}}$ is the valve coefficient and $K_T$ the level-control gain; the make-up vanishes identically after $t_{\mathrm{fail}}$ (Eq. (2.2)).

**Gravity-driven flow.** The tank drains into the sump by gravity,

$$
Q_{\mathrm{grav}}(t)=C_v^{\mathrm{g}}\,\nu_{\mathrm{grav}}(t)\sqrt{\bigl(H_t(t)+\Delta z-H_p(t)\bigr)^{+}}\cdot\mathbf{1}\bigl[H_t>H_t^{\min}\bigr],
\tag{2.6}
$$

$$
\nu_{\mathrm{grav}}(t)=\mathrm{clip}\bigl(K_P\bigl(H_P^{\mathrm{set}}-H_p\bigr),\,0,\,1\bigr),
\tag{2.7}
$$

**Circulating pumps.** The rated circulating-water flow is calibrated from the unit capacity and the design temperature rise [37]:

$$
m_{cw,0}=k_{cw}\,P_{\max},\qquad k_{cw}=0.0295\ \mathrm{m^3 s^{-1} MW^{-1}}
\tag{2.8}
$$

where the coefficient embodies a design temperature rise of $\Delta T_d = 8\ \mathrm{K}$ [37]; a falling sump level erodes the submergence and the circulation derates piecewise:

$$
m_{cw}(t)=m_{cw,0}\cdot s\bigl(H_p\bigr),\qquad
s(H)=\begin{cases}
0, & H\le H_s,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{H-H_s}{\Delta H_s}, & H_s<H<H_s+\Delta H_s,\\[6pt]
1, & H\ge H_s+\Delta H_s,
\end{cases}
\tag{2.9}
$$

where $H_s$ is the minimum submergence depth and $\Delta H_s$ the derating band; the pump trip at $H_s$ is latched.

**Cavitation margin.** The cavitation margin is monitored through the available net positive suction head:

$$
\mathrm{NPSH}_a=\frac{p_{\mathrm{atm}}-p_{\mathrm{vap}}}{\rho_w g}+H_p-h_{f,0}\Bigl(\frac{m_{cw}}{m_{cw,0}}\Bigr)^{2},
\tag{2.10}
$$

where $h_{f,0}$ is the rated suction friction loss (values in Supplementary S1.2); the submergence law of Eq. (2.9), rather than cavitation, is the dominant pump failure criterion.

**Circulating losses.** The circulating losses consist of evaporation, blowdown, and drift [37]:

$$
Q_{\mathrm{evap}}=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cond}}}{h_{fg}\,\rho_w},\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{bd}}=\beta\,Q_{\mathrm{evap}},\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{drift}}=\alpha_d\,m_{cw},
\tag{2.11}
$$

$$
Q_{\mathrm{loss}}=Q_{\mathrm{evap}}+Q_{\mathrm{bd}}+Q_{\mathrm{drift}},
\tag{2.12}
$$

where $Q_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is the condenser heat load, $h_{fg}$ the latent heat, $\beta$ the blowdown ratio, and $\alpha_d$ the drift fraction; the total loss lies within the 2–3% range of GB/T 50102 [37].

**Mass balance.** The pump intake and the cooling-tower return approximately cancel in the closed loop, and the two levels obey

$$
\frac{dH_t}{dt}=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{make}}-Q_{\mathrm{grav}}}{A_T},\qquad
\frac{dH_p}{dt}=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{grav}}-Q_{\mathrm{loss}}}{A_P}.
\tag{2.13}
$$

**Water temperature.** The tower outlet temperature is prescribed by the wet-bulb temperature and the approach; a reduced circulation degrades the tower heat exchange, and the inlet drifts toward the return temperature with mixing coefficient $\chi$:

$$
T_{\mathrm{in}}=\begin{cases}
T_{\mathrm{to}}+\chi\Bigl(1-\min\bigl(1,\tfrac{m_{cw}}{m_{cw,0}}\bigr)\Bigr)\bigl(T_{\mathrm{ret}}-T_{\mathrm{to}}\bigr)^{+}, & m_{cw}>0,\\[6pt]
T_{\mathrm{ret}}, & m_{cw}=0\ (\text{stagnant sump rewarming}),
\end{cases}
\tag{2.14}
$$

**Initialization.** The initial levels follow from fault-free forward integration from the target levels (Supplementary S1.2).

### 2.3.2 Condenser and low-pressure turbine model

**Condenser heat transfer.** The saturation pressure of water follows the Antoine equation (valid 1–100 °C). The condenser uses the ε-NTU method [38] — condensation implying a zero heat-capacity ratio — with the conductance scaling as $UA \propto m_{cw}^{\,n}$, $n = 0.8$, after the Dittus–Boelter turbulent correlation [20]:

$$
\log_{10} p_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{mmHg}}=A-\frac{B}{C+T},\qquad
A=8.07131,\ B=1730.63,\ C=233.426,\ \ p_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.1333224\,p_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{mmHg}}\ \mathrm{kPa}.
\tag{2.15}
$$

$$
C_{cw}=\rho_w c_p\,m_{cw},\qquad
UA=UA_0\Bigl(\frac{m_{cw}}{m_{cw,0}}\Bigr)^{n},\qquad
\varepsilon=1-e^{-\mathrm{NTU}},\qquad
\mathrm{NTU}=\frac{UA}{C_{cw}},
\tag{2.16}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{ret}}=T_{\mathrm{in}}+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cond}}}{C_{cw}},\qquad
T_{\mathrm{sat}}=T_{\mathrm{in}}+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cond}}}{\varepsilon C_{cw}},\qquad
p_b=p_{\mathrm{sat}}\bigl(T_{\mathrm{sat}}\bigr).
\tag{2.17}
$$

**Zero-flow branch.** With zero flow the back pressure is set to a fallback of $3\,p_b^{\mathrm{trip}}$ (capped at 50 kPa), always above the trip setting — the unit trips promptly, the chain is not interrupted.

**Back-calibration of the nominal conductance.** $UA_0$ is back-calculated from the rated design point — the design back pressure, the design inlet temperature, and the rated heat load $Q_{\mathrm{cond},0} = \lambda_Q P_{g,0}$:

$$
\varepsilon_0=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cond},0}}{C_{cw,0}\bigl(T_{\mathrm{sat}}(p_{b,0})-T_{\mathrm{in},0}\bigr)},\qquad
NTU_0=-\ln\bigl(1-\varepsilon_0\bigr),\qquad
UA_0=NTU_0\,C_{cw,0}.
\tag{2.18}
$$

where $\lambda_Q$ is the condenser-load-to-output ratio including the cycle heat rate. The 4 K nominal terminal difference is a design intent, not enforced; the actual value follows from the self-consistent calibration (Supplementary S1.3).

**Back-pressure output correction.** The unit output is derated linearly with the back pressure above its design value:

$$
k_p(t)=\Bigl(1-\gamma\bigl(p_b(t)-p_{b,0}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr)^{+},\qquad
\gamma=0.02\ \mathrm{kPa^{-1}},
\tag{2.19}
$$

Each kPa of excess back pressure reduces the output by about 2%; the actual output $P_g = P_{g,0}\,k_p$ is limited by the runback command of Eq. (2.26).

**High-back-pressure protection.** The unit trips when the back pressure persists above $p_b^{\mathrm{trip}}$ (about three times the design value) for a set delay, latched; if the pumps have already tripped, immediately at the setting.

**Heat-load update.** Before the trip the heat load follows the actual output, $Q_{\mathrm{cond}} = \lambda_Q P_{\mathrm{aff}} \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kW}$, decreasing with the derating; under a commanded runback the actual output is the minimum of the command and the derating, Eq. (2.26), so a faster runback slows the storage consumption through the same law. After the trip the residual exhaust heat decays at $Q_{\mathrm{cond},0}/20$ and vanishes within about 20 s.

### 2.3.3 Early warning indicators

Three early warning indicators are defined on the cooling-water chain, following the framework of [25] with the physical quantities redefined for the cooling-water domain (Table 1).

**Table 1 Cooling-water early-warning indicators and their gas-domain analogues**

| Indicator (this work) | Definition | Gas-domain analogue [25] |
|---|---|---|
| **CWR** (cooling-water reserve) | Stored volume available before the circulating pumps trip, in the make-up tank and the collection sump | ALP (available line-pack) |
| **UET** (unit endurance time) | Remaining time before the affected unit reaches the high-back-pressure or pump protection | AET (ailable escape time) |
| **SUET** (static unit endurance time) | Initial buffer window at the failure instant ($t = 0$) | SAET (static AET) |

The cooling-water reserve is the stored volume above the withdrawal limits:

$$
\mathrm{CWR}(t)=A_T\bigl(H_t-H_t^{\min}\bigr)+A_P\bigl(H_p-H_s\bigr),
\tag{2.20}
$$

The unit endurance time under a linear ramp-down of the affected unit at rate $r$ is

$$
\mathrm{UET}(r)=\begin{cases}
t_{\mathrm{trip}}(r), & \text{protection trips the unit first},\\[2pt]
1/r, & \text{ramp-down completes first},
\end{cases}
\tag{2.21}
$$

and its value at zero ramp rate defines the static indicator:

$$
\mathrm{SUET}=\mathrm{UET}(0),
\tag{2.22}
$$

The forward integration in Eq. (2.21) couples the two submodels by explicit Euler stepping (1 s); $t_{\mathrm{trip}}$ is the first instant at which a protection criterion is met.

**Critical ramp-down rate.** The same feedback implies a critical ramp-down rate $r^{*}$ that maximizes the operation time: below it the unit is tripped by protection after buffer depletion, above it the unit can shut down actively first. This is the physical basis of the proactive control below; the numerical example is Fig. 6 in Chapter 4.

## 2.4 Cross-domain early warning mechanism

The cooling-water model yields a physical time margin, the SUET window; this section defines the information architecture that converts it into usable control time — the cross-domain warning channel and the slow-fast gap it exploits.

### 2.4.1 Cross-domain early warning channel

The information layer is organized into three domains and three levels (Fig. 1, right), following the hierarchical monitoring of [13]. Its key design is the cross-domain early-warning channel: a head violation detected by the municipal SCADA is pushed directly to power dispatch, bypassing level-by-level reporting, with zero latency.

The detection criterion of the channel is

$$
t_{\mathrm{warn}}=\inf\bigl\{\,t:\ H_{\mathrm{muni}}(t)<H_{\min}\,\bigr\}=t_{\mathrm{fail}},
\tag{2.23}
$$

The warning threshold equals the make-up failure threshold of Eq. (2.2), measured at the plant intake head — a standard SCADA quantity. The warning coincides with the failure instant, is latched, and once delivered enables the runback and the reserve pre-start.

The threshold, trigger, and grading choices are deliberate and justified in Supplementary S1.4: the coincident threshold reuses existing telemetry with no tuning degree of freedom (a warning implies a genuine supply interruption); the head trigger, though later than the source stop by 1.25–9.00 h, anchors to the failure criterion and yields the conservative lead-time bound; ungraded levels add nothing under a deterministic outage. The conclusions of Chapters 4 and 5 are lower bounds in all respects.

### 2.4.2 Slow-fast timescale gap

Fig. 3 summarizes the causal structure: the buffer depletes on minutes-scale dynamics (Eq. (2.13)) while the back pressure rises until a protection acts, whereas the detection–warning–control chain responds in seconds; the SUET window between them is the time available for active control. Passively the reserves start only after the trip, bound by their start-up rates; actively they pre-start at $t = 0$ and the units land softly by runback within the window, eliminating the deficit (Supplementary Table S2; Chapter 4).

**Fig. 3 Slow-fast timescale gap and the early warning mechanism**: upper axis, water-side slow dynamics, in which the stored water of the tank and the sump depletes after the municipal pressure failure ($t = 0$) and the back pressure rises gradually until the protection trips the unit at the end of the SUET window; the ICS detects the municipal head violation and delivers the cross-domain warning to the power dispatcher with zero latency (fast dynamics). ## 2.5 Power system model

The power-side model quantifies the deficit that a cooling-water failure imposes on the power system. It consists of a DC network model and a unit response model.

### 2.5.1 DC network model

The network is the IEEE 118-bus test system [39] with the DC formulation [40–42], phase angles referenced to the base case; the line flows are linear functions of the nodal injections, with the power transfer distribution factors (PTDF) computed by a Python implementation of the MATPOWER routine [41]:

$$
P_{f,\ell,t}=\sum_n \Pi_{\ell n}\bigl(\bigl[A_g P_{g,t}\bigr]_n-P_{d,n}\bigr),\qquad \ell=1,\dots,L,
\tag{2.24}
$$

where $\Pi$ is the PTDF matrix and $A_g$ is the generator-bus incidence matrix.

The line limits are derived from the base-case flow with a short-time overload factor $\kappa_r$ (1.5, customary 125–150%) and a floor — the public case118 attaches no ratings (rate-A zero for all 186 branches):

$$
\overline{F}_{\ell}=\max\bigl(\kappa_r\,\bigl|P_{f,\ell}^{\mathrm{base}}\bigr|,\ F_{\min}\bigr),\qquad
\kappa_r=1.5,\quad F_{\min}=50\ \mathrm{MW}.
\tag{2.25}
$$

The constructed limits enter only through the transfer constraints and the overload metric; a larger $\kappa_r$ only enlarges the LP feasible set, so the zero-deficit conclusions are conservative in this respect.

### 2.5.2 Unit response model

In the ICS event simulation, the output of the affected unit is

$$
P_{\mathrm{aff}}(t)=P_{\mathrm{aff},0}\cdot\min\bigl(u_{\mathrm{cmd}}(t),\,k_p(t)\bigr)\quad(\text{before the trip}),\qquad
\frac{du_{\mathrm{cmd}}}{dt}=-\min\bigl(\rho_{rb},\ \tfrac{r_{\mathrm{slow}}}{P_{\mathrm{aff},0}}\bigr)\quad(\text{after the warning}),
\tag{2.26}
$$

The output is scaled by the smaller of the runback command and the back-pressure derating factor of Eq. (2.19); the command rate is capped at $\rho_{rb}$ and bounded by the slow start-up share, so the fastest complete runback takes 15 min (without a warning, $u_{\mathrm{cmd}} \equiv 1$).

The remaining units provide two tiers of reserves:

$$
R_{\mathrm{spin}}=\rho_r\,P_{\mathrm{aff},0},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{slow}}=P_{\mathrm{aff},0},\qquad
r_{\mathrm{slow}}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{aff},0}}{T_{\mathrm{slow}}},
\qquad \rho_r=0.20,\ T_{\mathrm{slow}}=900\ \mathrm{s},
\tag{2.27}
$$

The spinning reserve is immediately available, while the slow start-up reserve ramps at the rate $r_{\mathrm{slow}}$ and reaches full deployment in about 15 min.

The timing reference $t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of the reserve start-up is the dividing line between the two cases:

$$
t_{\mathrm{ref}}=\begin{cases}t_{\mathrm{warn}}=0, & \text{with warning},\\ t_{\mathrm{trip}}, & \text{no warning},\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathrm{deficit}(t)=\Bigl(P_{\mathrm{aff},0}-P_{\mathrm{aff}}(t)-R_{\mathrm{spin}}-\min\bigl(C_{\mathrm{slow}},\,r_{\mathrm{slow}}(t-t_{\mathrm{ref}})\bigr)\Bigr)^{+}.
\tag{2.28}
$$

Under active operation $t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ equals the warning instant, under passive the trip instant; the deficit is the positive part of the loss not covered by the two tiers.

The two tiers total $1.2\,P_{\mathrm{aff},0}$ and cover the maximum unit loss, so any deficit arises purely from timing. The passive peak before the slow tier arrives is analytic, $(1-\rho_r)\,P_{\mathrm{aff},0}$ — 485.6 MW for the 607 MW unit at $\rho_r = 0.20$; under active operation the same reserves pre-ramp from $t = 0$. The design isolates the value of the cross-domain information in one quantity, the deficit.

## 2.6 Proactive resilience optimization

The network-wide proactive control is a single linear program [43] over a 300 min horizon with step $\Delta t = 5\ \mathrm{min}$; the decision variables are the unit outputs $P_g[i,t]$, the deficit slack $d_t \ge 0$, and the overload slack $o_{\ell,t} \ge 0$. The load is held at its base-case value — the conservative choice (Supplementary S1.5). The objective minimizes the energy deficit and penalizes overload:

$$
\min\ \sum_t d_t\,\Delta t\ +\ w\sum_{\ell,t} o_{\ell,t},\qquad w=5.
\tag{2.29}
$$

**Constraints.** Five classes are imposed — nodal balance (2.30), capacity (2.31), ramping (2.32), power flow (2.33), and passive causality (2.34):

$$
\text{(balance)}\quad \sum_i P_g[i,t]+d_t=P_L,\qquad\forall t;
\tag{2.30}
$$

$$
\text{(capacity)}\quad 0\le P_g[i,t]\le
\begin{cases}
P_{\mathrm{avail},i}(t), & i\in S_{\mathrm{aff}},\\[2pt]
\min\bigl(P_{g0,i}+\rho_{\mathrm{res}}(P_{\max,i}-P_{g0,i}),\ P_{\max,i}\bigr), & i\notin S_{\mathrm{aff}},
\end{cases}
\tag{2.31}
$$

$$
\text{(ramping)}\quad \bigl|P_g[i,t]-P_g[i,t-1]\bigr|\le R_i\,\Delta t,\qquad
R_i=\rho_{\mathrm{ramp}}P_{\max,i},\ \ \rho_{\mathrm{ramp}}=0.01\ \mathrm{min^{-1}},\quad i\notin S_{\mathrm{aff}}
\tag{2.32}
$$

The constraint applies at $t = 0$ relative to the base-case output. All proactive chains use $\rho_{\mathrm{ramp}} = 0.01\ \mathrm{min^{-1}}$ [44]; the N-k scans adopt 0.02 (the Table 4 note). The affected units are exempt, following Eq. (2.35).

$$
\text{(power flow)}\quad \bigl|P_{f,\ell,t}\bigr|\le\overline{F}_{\ell}+o_{\ell,t},\qquad \forall \ell,t
\tag{2.33}
$$

The slack makes the constraint soft; under the hard mode ($o \equiv 0$) the deficit degenerates to the minimum load shedding. The weight $w$ shapes only the overload slack — the SP deficit stays at zero over $w \in \{1, 5, 20\}$ (`w_pa_checks.json`).

$$
\text{(passive causality)}\quad P_g[i,t]\le P_{g0,i},\qquad i\notin S_{\mathrm{aff}},\ \ t<t_{\mathrm{ft}},
\tag{2.34}
$$

where $t_{\mathrm{ft}}$ is the first trip among the affected units: passively the remaining units cannot raise output before the deficit appears, actively they ramp from $t = 0$.

**Available trajectories.** The available output trajectory of the affected units $i \in S_{\mathrm{aff}}$ is

$$
P_{\mathrm{avail},i}(t)=\begin{cases}
P_{g0,i}\Bigl(1-\dfrac{(t-\tau_i)^{+}}{T_i}\Bigr)^{+}, & \text{SP/DP (linear soft landing)},\\[8pt]
P_{g0,i}\cdot\mathbf{1}\bigl[t<\tau_i+t_{\mathrm{trip},i}\bigr], & \text{PA (sudden drop along the hydraulic trajectory)},
\end{cases}
\tag{2.35}
$$

where $\tau_i$ is the municipal failure offset of unit $i$, zero by default in the co-located case; the trip instant follows from Eq. (2.21) at $r = 0$.

**Control times.** The control times of the two proactive strategies are

$$
T_i^{\mathrm{SP}}=\mathrm{SUET}_i,\qquad T_i^{\mathrm{DP}}=\alpha\cdot\mathrm{SUET}_i,\qquad \alpha=1.5.
\tag{2.36}
$$

The dynamic proactive control of [25] prolongs the control time iteratively; this study extends it in a single step, $T_i = \alpha\,\mathrm{SUET}_i$, whose conservatism is characterized in Chapter 5.

**Impact metrics.** The impact is quantified by

$$
P_{\mathrm{def}}^{\max}=\max_t d_t,\qquad
E_{\mathrm{def}}=\sum_t d_t\,\Delta t,
\tag{2.37}
$$

that is, the peak and the energy deficit, both aggregating Eq. (2.28) over time.

The municipal failure offset decouples the intake position, which shifts the crisis origin, from the cooling buffer, which sets the window length. Isolated crises decompose into independent single-unit programs — peaks by the maximum, energies by the sum (layouts in Chapter 5).

# 3 Case Study and Setup

This chapter instantiates the models of Chapter 2 on an auditable test bed — a real city network, a public standard power system, deterministic coupling rules — so the failure dynamics are credible and every number auditable.

## 3.1 Test systems

**Municipal water system.** The water side uses the D-town benchmark network [29, 30] (`data/DTOWN.inp`): 399 junctions, one reservoir (R1, total head 80 m), seven district tanks, 443 pipes, 11 pumps, five valves, and base demands of 422.3 L/s under five daily patterns with a real diurnal swing [45]. The simulation uses EPANET 2.2 extended-period simulation with pressure-driven demand [31, 32] (15 min step, 72 h window); the failure and health thresholds are 28 m and 32 m. The single-reservoir structure caps the severity: the full stop of R1 is a deterministic extreme, the upper tier of the scenario matrix.

**Power system.** The power side uses the IEEE 118-bus system [39]: 118 buses, 186 branches, 54 units, 4,242 MW load; 19 units carry output (4,377 MW), 35 are zero-output dispatch points. The largest unit sits at bus 89 with $P_g/P_{\max}$ = 607/707 MW.

## 3.2 Water–power coupling

**Coupling scope.** Two physical facts rule out coupling every unit to every node: 35 of the 54 unit entries have $P_g = 0$ (synchronous condensers or placeholder dispatch points, no cooling-water demand), and 51 of the 399 junctions have zero base demand (pure through-flow nodes, no physical intake). The coupling set therefore converges to six large condensing units paired with six real supply nodes.

**Hard filters.** The convergence is made permanent by three hard filters — positive base demand, a fault-free 24 h minimum head above 32 m, and a pressure-failure event within the 72 h window under the full stop of R1 — fixed in `coupling_map.py` and registered in `results/muni/coupling_map.json`. The six designated nodes (J102, J97, J198, J5, J217, J177, all in DMA1) pass all three, 6/6.

**Deterministic pairing.** The pairing is a rank-to-rank mapping: the intake nodes ordered by failure time (J102 1.25 h to J177 9.00 h) matched to the units ordered by output (buses 89, 80, 10, 66, 65, 26). The six units carry 2,631 MW, 60.1% of the on-line output; the remaining 13 on-line units (1,746 MW) are uncoupled, assumed to retain independent cooling sources — isolating the designated common-origin event. # 4 Results

The results follow the failure chain from the municipal network to the boundary of proactive control: the water-side propagation, the warning value, the strategy comparison, and the boundary of sufficiency.

**Reporting conventions.** Three caliber choices recur; every number below is reported under the chain it belongs to:

- **Municipal-failure calibers.** B-ST (full stop of R1 at $t = 0$) is the caliber of Supplementary Figs. S1 and S2 and of the coupling registration; B-RT (linear head decay over 3 h from $t = 6$ h) feeds the full-order cascade and the closed water balance of Chapter 5.
**SUET calibers.** The heat-load update law of §2.3.2 is implemented identically in all chains: the warning-indicator (1 s) and mechanism (4 s) chains give 92.4 min, the ICS event chain (8 s macro step) 92.6 min — a 0.2% integration spread; a single SUET caliber is quoted throughout.
- **Deficit chains.** Single-unit results come from the ICS event simulation (two-tier reserves plus runback), multi-unit and N-k results from the proactive-control LP; the reserve semantics differ, the numbers are not directly comparable.

## 4.1 Water-side propagation: from network depressurization to unit trip

**Network-wide depressurization (B-ST).** After R1 [29, 30] stops at $t = 0$, the city holds its pressure only through the seven district tanks: of the 399 nodes, 289 (72.4%) fall below 28 m within 72 h (quantiles in §5.1). Supplementary Fig. S2 shows the collapse — 9.5% below threshold in the instantaneous initial solution, 50.1% failed by +12.5 h, the failed fraction non-monotone with the diurnal demand (peaks 69.2%/66.7%/71.9%, troughs 8.3–37.3%, ending 56.4%) — a three-day progressive collapse.

**Staggered failure at the coupled offtakes (Supplementary Fig. S1).** Under the same fault the six coupled nodes fail at 1.25–9.00 h under B-ST: a crisis beginning at one instant unfolds at the plants with an hour-scale offset — the basis of the designated-placement study and the cascade scenario. Under B-RT they fail at 33.5–35.0 h, a six-of-six consistency check (`full_coupling_boundary.json`).

**Availability of backup make-up water.** Over 72 h under B-RT the six nodes' rated demand totals 24,806 m³, 71.4% delivered (per-node 51.8%–98.6%, anti-correlated with the failure order); the closed water balance of Chapter 5 gives 71.2% under the true trajectory.

**Single-unit timeline (B-ST, bus 89).** Fig. 5 traces the complete chain: the make-up tank empties at 5.2 min, the sump becoming the only buffer; at 71.9 min the sump enters the submergence-derating band and the circulation derates linearly; at 93.4 min the back pressure crosses 15 kPa with $m_{cw}$ at 35.9% and the sump at 1.38 m — the 1.2 m pump-trip level never reached, the flow derating tripping the protection first. The unit trips at 93.4 min, 92.4 min after the failure (the SUET caliber), the output falling from 607 MW to zero (Supplementary S3.2).

A municipal water stop does not translate directly into a unit stop: the on-site storage delays the trip by about 92 min (93.6 min under a 600 s ramp, Chapter 5) — the intermediate process missing from the direct mappings [12] and the physical source of the exploitable margin.

**SUET calibers.** All chains share the heat-load update law of §2.3.2, the load falling along the derating. The warning-indicator (1 s), mechanism (4 s), and ICS event (8 s macro step) chains agree within their tolerances — 92.4, 92.4, and 92.6 min — and a single SUET caliber is quoted throughout.

**Critical ramp-down rate.** The window is not fixed. Sweeping $r$ over full-power durations of 600–24,000 s (`critical_ramp_example.json`), the running time peaks at $T_{\max} = 183.3$ min at exactly $r^{*} = 0.545\%\,P_g/\mathrm{min}$ — 1.98 times the 92.4 min SUET. Slower rates end in a forced trip with 4–9% of the storage left; faster rates allow an active shutdown. The mechanism is the derating self-protection feedback — a lower output cuts the heat load, the losses, and the storage consumption — so the window is an actively extendable quantity, the physical basis of the dynamic strategy (aligned with Fig. 3 of [25]).

## 4.2 Effect of the early warning at the unit level

This is the central comparison of the paper. The single-unit fault is identical in both cases, a step loss of the municipal supply at bus 89 with $t_{\mathrm{fault}} = 60$ s; the only difference is whether the power side receives the warning (Fig. 7, `ics_warning_compare.json`).

**Without warning (passive).** After the buffer is exhausted, the unit trips at $t = 93.6$ min as the back pressure climbs across the 15.2 kPa limit, losing 607 MW at once; the reserves start only at the trip. The peak deficit is 485.6 MW, about 11.4% of the 4,242 MW system load [39]; the energy deficit is 49.1 MWh, the window 93.6–105.5 min (backfilled in about 12 min).

**With warning (active).** The head crossing is detected at $t = 1.1$ min — four seconds after the fault, within one macro step, zero latency — triggering a runback matched to the reserve ramping (zero output in 15 min) plus the pre-start of both reserve tiers. The back pressure stays at or below the 5.0 kPa design value, never approaching the 15 kPa protection; the sump bottoms at 2.67 m, far above the 1.2 m trip level. Both the deficit and the trip count are zero.

**Mechanism of the elimination.** A single cross-domain message removes the impact completely — the quantitative realization of the time-scale separation of Chapter 2 (Table S2): the buffer provides a 92 min handling window, and the warning moves the reserve start from its end to its beginning. **The elimination is over-determined, not fine-tuned:** the window exceeds the full deployment sixfold — a margin that the boundary scans of Chapters 4–5 consume on the ramping and capacity axes.

## 4.3 Strategy comparison under the common-origin scenario

The multi-unit level uses the common-origin scenario: the six units at buses 89/80/10/66/65/26 in crisis simultaneously under CO, solved as a joint LP with DC power flow over 300 min (`p6_strategy_compare.json`), aligned with the city-level case of [25]. The six units hold 2,631 MW with SUETs of 92.4/121.8/130.0/151.5/151.9/192.9 min.

**Passive baseline (PA).** The six units trip in sequence at their SUETs and form two deficit episodes: 334.9 MW over 95–105 min, when bus 89 trips with the reserves still cold, and 143.5 MW over 125–130 min, when bus 80 trips with the reserves partly on-line; the later trips land on the accumulated reserves. The energy deficit is 59.6 MWh, the maximum overload 165.8 MW. The registered caliber trips each unit as a step; the physical derating trajectory lowers the metrics only mildly, to 324.9 MW and 57.0 MWh (`w_pa_checks.json`) — slightly overestimating the passive deficit, a conservative comparison.

**Proactive strategies (SP and DP).** The warning triggers a soft landing — a linear reduction to zero over SUET (SP) or $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{SUET}$ (DP) from $t = 0$ — while the remaining units pre-ramp. Both deficit metrics are zero, the maximum overload falling to 36.8 MW, a normal transfer within the ramping corridor. The windows are ample here — SP alone reaches zero, DP coincides with SP; the boundary follows later in this chapter, the mechanism in Chapter 6.

**Designated placement (DISP).** Under the designated staggered placement (DISP, $\tau = [0, 0.5, 2.5, 2.5, 5.75, 7.75]$ h) the passive energy deficit falls to 43.7 MWh (−26.6%), the peak staying at 334.9 MW as the first common-origin cluster dominates (Δ = 0%); the system-level analysis follows in Chapter 5 (Fig. 11).

## 4.4 Robustness boundary of proactive control

**N-k contingency scan (Fig. 9).** To draw the applicability boundary, the six designated units ($\Sigma P_g = 2{,}631$ MW) are scanned combinatorially: k = 1–6 fully enumerated, each k contributing the worst top-k subset plus three fixed-seed random subsets, 24 items, each solved under PA and SP over 300 min. Three regularities emerge (Table 4):

- **Absorption for all k.** SP is at zero deficit in all 24 subsets, including the full k = 6 set (2,631 MW): the 92.4–192.9 min windows suffice for any combination of up to six units.
- **Passive floor and saturation.** The worst-subset PA peak jumps from 91.1 MW at k = 1 to 146–155 MW for k ≥ 2 and stops growing; the energy deficit saturates at 12.2–12.9 MWh. The deficit is capped by the ramping of the remaining units and the line limits, decoupled from the lost-output magnitude (607 to 2,631 MW).
- **Effect of subset composition.** PA is deficit-free in 11 of the 24 subsets; every non-zero subset contains bus 89 or the 89 + 80 pair (a double crisis within 0.5 h), the random subsets excluding bus 89 being absorbed — the deficit depends on the overlap structure of the failure instants, not only on the magnitude.

**Full-order cascade.** A 72 h cascade in the B-RT failure order ($t_{\mathrm{muni}}$ + SUET as the absolute trip instant) turns all six units into events at 35.0–38.2 h, merging into a single cluster ($T_0 = 33.5$ h, 2,631 MW). The within-cluster joint LP gives PA 134.8 MW / 11.2 MWh while SP is zero; SIM coincides, and the per-event lower bound SUM gives only 8.6 MW / 0.7 MWh. The 3.2 h stagger span is fully covered by the cooling windows (SUET 1.5–3.2 h): the cascade degenerates into a near-simultaneous failure, the per-unit decomposition underestimating it by a factor of about 16 — the common-origin cluster is dominant, and proactive control still covers it completely.

# 5 Sensitivity Analysis

This chapter tests the main conclusions and their applicability conditions on three axes — where the intake sits, how much reserve the remaining units must hold, and how robust the water-side buffer window is — complementary to the failure-scale axis of Chapter 4.

The time origin of each crisis is set by the primary buffer, the city-side district tanks, whose depletion depends strongly on the intake position [47, 48]. ## 5.3 Robustness and parameter sensitivity of the water-side buffer window

**Failure rate (S03).** A 600 s ramped failure widens the window: the bus 89 trip moves from 92.4 to 93.6 min. Under B-RT the depressurization unfolds over hours (nodes failing 27.5–29.0 h, Chapter 4) — B-ST is a conservative lower bound, as the closed water balance verifies.

That conservativeness is examined through the closed water balance (`closed_water_balance.json`): the make-up valves driven by the true B-RT trajectory, the flow multiplied by the pressure-driven share $f(p)$ [34] (Supplementary S3.4).

**Caliber conservativeness.** The closed-trajectory SUET is never smaller than B-ST: bus 89 rises from 92.4 min to 1,729.1 min (about 28.8 h, the $P_g$ caliber; 1,713.9 at $P_{\max}$), and across the six units the closed SUETs span 1,713.9–1,911.6 min (median 1,784.2) — nearly a factor of 19. The 92–193 min windows are lower bounds; under realistic decay the margins are tens of hours.

**Rated-cap make-up stress.** The pre-failure equilibrium draws up to 452.5 L/s at the largest unit against a 20.0 L/s rated connection, a 23-fold gap. The stress question — whether a cap-restricted plant would drain its buffer ahead of the municipal latch — is answered negatively (`makeup_cap_sensitivity.json`): all six units still trip only after their own municipal failure, the windows shrink by only 14–22%, and the order is preserved. A static estimate would predict a collapse to a third of the window; the coupled dynamics give a 22% shrink (Supplementary S3.3).

**Heat-load robustness and water closure.** All six units trip under the 72 h closed trajectory under both heat-load calibers; the balance delivers 381,955 of 536,601 m³ (71.2%), cross-checking the 71.4% of Chapter 4, the 28 m gate deferring the closed SUET to tens of hours.

The water-side SUETs rest on a handful of design and ambient parameters, swept one at a time around the nominal configuration under the unified heat-load semantics, with the UA rating re-scaled to the rated point at every level (`suet_sensitivity.json`). # 6 Discussion

This chapter distills the mechanisms behind Chapters 4 and 5, states the engineering implications, and delimits the scope.

## 6.1 Mechanistic insights

The buffer tiers and their positions determine both the failure path and the time margin. The same source stop activates two buffers in sequence. The district tanks are hour-scale and position-dependent (0–67 h); the plant cooling-water reserve is minute-scale and position-independent (SUET 92.4–192.9 min). The primary buffer sets the crisis origin, the secondary the handling window. A gas network stores its linepack inside the network itself [25]; here the buffer is lumped in the plant, and the municipal network is only a pressure boundary. The boundary-generator architecture of Chapter 2 follows directly.

The buffer window is actively extendable. The critical-rate example exposes a derating self-protection feedback: ramping down at $r^{*} = 0.545\%\,P_g/\mathrm{min}$ extends the running time to 1.98 times the SUET (183.3 min). Slower rates end in a forced trip with 4–9% of the storage left; faster rates shut down earlier. The feedback buys tens of minutes of handling time at no capital cost, on the strength of the condenser and protection characteristics alone.

The failure mode is gradual degradation, not a sudden flow loss. The circulation derates linearly, the back pressure climbs, and the protection trips on the limit crossing — a two-stage grid-side signature. This signature is the precondition for early warning: the intervention window opens before the protection acts [49]. It also contrasts with cascade criticality, where outage risk grows through slow margin erosion [26–28]; here the erosion is explicit, quantified, and once warned against, fully absorbable.

## 6.2 Engineering implications

The cheapest resilience gain is to open the warning channel and tune the runback to the critical rate. Admitting municipal node heads into power dispatch as an external alarm needs no new sensors and no tuning — the threshold equals the failure criterion, 28 m. The ceiling of this single interface is the entire single-unit deficit, 485.6 MW and 49.1 MWh, avoided. The runback rate should sit close to $r^{*}$ (0.55% $P_g$/min here); faster brings no benefit, slower ends in a forced trip. Plans graded by SUET can be pre-built offline per unit capacity.

Once the warning moves the reserve start to the beginning of the window, the limit becomes reserve adequacy and ramping under a common-origin loss. Plant clusters sharing a water source should audit their regional spinning reserves and ramping rates [44, 50, 51] against a simultaneous loss of the full coupled set, 2,631 MW. This is the paper's most direct recommendation for grid planning. SP is zero for every k = 1–6 subset; the passive deficit saturates at 12–13 MWh.

Intake dispersal is the second-best remedy, and only without the warning. The designated placement staggers 0.5–7.75 h and trims the passive energy deficit by 26.6% without trimming the peak. With the warning in place, the placement value drops to zero: the warning value is robust, dispersion the fallback. For utilities extending supply-side redundancy, this ranks the interventions [47, 48].

## 6.3 Scope and limitations

The conclusions rest on conservative setup choices rather than optimistic ones. Degenerate subjects — 35 of 54 generators at $P_g = 0$ and 51 of 399 nodes with zero demand — were disclosed and hard-filtered, and the combinatorial scenarios take exactly the remaining six. The B-ST step caliber is a conservative lower bound: under the realistic trajectory the closed water balance puts the SUETs at tens of hours, so the margins only grow.

The coupling is deliberately one-directional. Only the water-to-power cascade is modeled; the reverse direction and a two-sided co-solution of water quantity are future work. The municipal network acts as a head boundary generator — the make-up demand never enters the city hydraulics. The synthetic pairing has precedent [12], admissibility guaranteed by the hard filters, and arbitrariness covered by Chapter 5. One-sidedness is what keeps the time-margin mechanism quantifiable [12, 13].

The information layer is a utilization channel, not a simulation object. The ICS formalizes the time gap into a cross-domain warning channel, analogous to the GDC-to-EPCC channel of [25]. Its own dynamics — latency, missed detection, tuning — are held ideal for the clean warning-versus-no-warning contrast and declared future work (Chapter 7). The model applies to conventional coal-fired units with circulating cooling and municipal make-up; once-through and seawater cooling fail differently. Within the resilience frameworks of [21–24], the study occupies the quantified-mechanism corner.
# 7 Conclusions

This paper addressed the water–power cyber-physical system in which the circulating cooling water of a power plant is drawn from the municipal supply network, modeling the complete fault-driven cooling-water process explicitly for the first time in an early-warning resilience study and converting the time margin into grid resilience through early-warning-driven proactive optimization. The demonstration on the designated six-pair coupling of IEEE-118 and D-town delivers the mechanism, the value quantification, and the boundary delimitation.

**The cooling buffer decouples the water stop from the unit trip by about 92 min.** The 92 min value is the conservative B-ST lower bound at bus 89; the six plants span 92.4–192.9 min. The failure mode is a gradual degradation, and a critical ramp-down rate at $0.55\%\,P_g/\mathrm{min}$ extends the running time to 2.0 times the SUET (183 min) through the derating self-protection feedback.

**The cross-domain early warning removes the deficit entirely.** Without the warning, a single-unit trip causes a peak deficit of 485.6 MW (about 11.4% of the load) and 49.1 MWh; with the warning, the runback soft landing plus the reserve pre-start brings both to zero, and under the common-origin six-unit scenario the passive 334.9 MW / 59.6 MWh likewise drops to zero under SP and DP.

**The applicability boundary lies on the power side, not the water side.** For every k = 1–6 subset (24 items) the warning-driven control stays at zero deficit, the passive energy deficit saturating at 12–13 MWh from k = 2 (peaks 146–155 MW), capped by the ramping and network limits. Under the full-order cascade, six events in a single cluster, SP remains at zero on both metrics.

**The intake position shapes only the passive response, and the water-side margin is conservative.** The failure times span 0–67 h; the designated placement staggers 0.5–7.75 h, trimming the passive energy deficit by 26.6% without trimming the peak, while proactive control is robust to the position. The closed water balance shows the B-ST results to be a conservative lower bound, with SUETs of tens of hours under the realistic trajectory.

**Future work.** Three directions follow: the bidirectional cascade and a spectrum of medium-severity municipal scenarios; AC power flow with frequency dynamics and geographic pairing with measured calibration; and the parameter map — the strict iterative DP, the reserve parameters, the DP ratio $\alpha$ — toward a unified early-warning framework for gas, water, and power.
